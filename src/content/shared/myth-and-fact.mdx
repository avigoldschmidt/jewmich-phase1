Is what you think about Chabad myth or fact? You may be surprised.

**Myth:** You have to be “Orthodox” to participate in programs offered by Chabad.  
**Fact:** Chabad is inclusive and non-judgmental, and programs are open to all. In fact, the vast majority of people who participate at Chabad are not “Orthodox.” The teachings of Chabad are imbued with Chassidic spirit and joy, but commitment to an Orthodox lifestyle is in no way a prerequisite to acceptance at Chabad functions.

**Myth:** Chabad’s goal is to make me Orthodox.  
**Fact:** Chabad is not out to make anyone Orthodox. Chabad is dedicated to engaging every Jew — regardless of background, affiliation, or personal level of observance — and providing resources to help increase Jewish knowledge, enthusiasm, and commitment. You are invited to explore Jewish religion, tradition, and practice in an open-minded atmosphere. Each person makes their own religious and lifestyle decisions at their own pace.

**Myth:** Chabad does not consider Reform, Conservative, or non-practicing Jews as “real” Jews.  
**Fact:** We prefer not to label other Jews, since labels tend to divide and create artificial barriers. The leader of Chabad worldwide, the Lubavitcher Rebbe of blessed memory, delivered a stinging rebuke to those who publicly belittled non-Orthodox Jews.

**Myth:** Chabad is financed by headquarters in New York.  
**Fact:** Each center is supported by the community it serves. Funding for local programs is generated locally. No dues are sent to international headquarters in New York — funds donated remain here in our community. We are not financially supported by central headquarters.
