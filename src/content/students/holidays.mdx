Celebrate the Jewish year with students at 715 Hill Street. Service and meal times change by season — reserve through the forms linked here, or [contact us](/contact) for current details. We do not post unverified times.

## High Holidays

Start the new year on a high note — Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur at Chabad.

Join warm, meaningful, easy-to-follow High Holiday services. **No fees. No tickets. Everyone is welcome.**

- **University of Michigan students** — use the student High Holiday registration.
- **Community members** — register on the [community holidays](/community/holidays) page.

You may also register by calling [734-995-3276](tel:+17349953276). High Holiday services are made possible by sponsors — if you would like to help, [donate](/donate) or ask the House.

## Sukkot

Come out under the stars and celebrate Sukkot in the Chabad sukkah. Student dinner sign-ups open when the season begins (Friday night and Saturday dinners are typical). Second-night dinners are listed when offered.

Registration is for **current U of M students**. Parents joining a student should be noted on the RSVP or call the House.

## Chanukah

Seasonal Chanukah gatherings include:

- **Latke Fest** — student Chanukah celebration (sign up when registration opens).
- **Mega Challah Bake** — a large challah-baking gathering when scheduled.

## Purim

Purim celebrations and the student Purim gift service run when available for the season. Use the Purim gift form linked from this page when it is open.

## Passover

Great food, good people, meaningful conversation, and a real Passover experience.

When Seders are open for the season, Michigan students register through the student Passover / Zeffy path; community members use the [community Passover](/community/holidays#passover) form. You can also reserve by calling [734-995-3276](tel:+17349953276).

**Common questions (from the live Passover page):**

- **Do only Orthodox people participate?** Not at all. Many people come looking for a meaningful, welcoming Jewish Passover experience.
- **What should I wear?** Whatever feels comfortable — casual or business casual is common.
- **How long does it last?** About an hour to an hour and a half reading and discussing the Haggadah, followed by matzah and a festive meal.
- **What kind of food?** Homemade Passover favorites — chicken soup, kugel, chicken, matzah, and more.

Shmurah matzah and meal add-ons are announced seasonally when available — ask the House if you need them.
